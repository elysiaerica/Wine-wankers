Spectrum

The anti-pretentious pour club — visual & verbal system

Every natural wine holds the light differently. This direction takes Nick's ubiquitous rainbow — present in every social post, painted on the bar wall, embedded in his store name — and translates it into the physics of refraction: white light through glass, splitting into visible spectrum. It's ownable, distinctive, and grounded in something real.

01 Strategic Rationale

Why this direction.

Three viable territories were evaluated against the brief, existing AI outputs, and the specific needs of Nick's community. Here's why Spectrum won.

Territory A

Suits, Hipsters, Everyone

An eclectic direction built around holding Nick's full demographic range. Strong concept but high execution risk — easy to become visually incoherent or collapse into generic "good taste" territory. No clear visual signature.

Territory B — Selected

The Rainbow Direction

Makes Nick's existing rainbow motif literal and ownable. Executed as optical science — prismatic refraction, visible spectrum — not millennial pastels. Completely unoccupied by all three prior AI outputs. Strong conceptual grounding with a single governing device.

Territory C

Natty Wine Eclectic

The visual world of actual natural wine labels — hand-drawn, lo-fi, warm. Valid territory but risks feeling like a label aesthetic, not a community brand. Better suited to the Chasing Rainbows store than the Wine Wankers identity.

Why Spectrum Works

The rainbow appears in nearly every post Nick writes. It's on the bar wall at The End. It's the name of his wine store. Yet none of the three prior outputs meaningfully engaged with it. This direction takes that underused asset and builds a complete visual system around it — not as decoration, but as the governing principle. The visible spectrum is universal, warm, inclusive, and genuinely distinctive in the natural wine space.

Usage rules

Never use all seven spectrum colours at equal weight. The spectrum appears as gradients, sequential bars, or 2–3 colour pairings. Yellow is the primary CTA accent. Red and violet are endpoints that anchor the visual range.

What this avoids

No sage green, terracotta, or millennial beige. No hot pink as dominant colour. No gradients that feel like tech startups. The palette should feel like a wine bar at golden hour, not a wellness brand.

03 Typography

Warm italic, sharp functional.

Four typefaces, four distinct roles. Syne owns the hero. Instrument Serif brings editorial warmth to section heads. Newsreader handles body. JetBrains Mono labels everything functional. Crushky is for social graphics only.

Syne ExtraBold 800 — Hero & H1

Hero title, key statement lines, page H1s. Lowercase. Tracking -0.02em. Line-height 0.9. NOT for section headings.

Instrument Serif Italic — Section heads

Page section h2/h3s. Italic. Warm, editorial, human. Chapter titles throughout site.

Newsreader — Body

Paragraphs, tasting notes, event copy. Italic for quotes and support lines.

JetBrains Mono — UI / Labels

Uppercase labels, nav, CTAs, captions. Monospace precision against warm serif.

Crushky Brush — Socials only

Instagram event posts, physical posters, social graphics. Never in web UI, body copy, or email.

09 Design System

Practical specifications.

Rules for developers and designers to maintain consistency without prescribing every element. The signature devices do the heavy lifting.

Layout approach

Generous margins (80px desktop, 24px mobile). 8px grid baseline. Maximum content width 1200px. Cards use border-radius: 2px or none. Asymmetric layouts preferred — left-anchored content, right-edge spectrum elements.

Border & line style

Subtle borders at 1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.07) on dark backgrounds. 1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.08) on light. No heavy strokes, no drop shadows. Separation through spacing, not boxing.

Texture & grain

Optional noise overlay at 2–4% opacity on dark sections. No gradients except the spectrum gradient itself. The scribble layer (see below) provides texture in dark contexts without disrupting readability.

Motion notes

Subtle fade-in on scroll (400ms). Spectrum bars animate in sequence (stagger 50ms per beam). No bounce, no elastic easing. Motion should feel like light changing, not UI reacting.

Signature Element — The Refraction Bars

Seven horizontal bars in spectrum order, staggered in width. This represents light refracting through glass — the rainbow emerging from a single source. The stagger is essential. Used as: corner device on dark pages, header element on Chasing Rainbows, event poster mark, email header, and the closing brand signature.

wine

Scribble Layer

Ghosted text at opacity: 0.035, font-size: 180–220px, Instrument Serif Italic. Use in dark sections only. Words: "WW", "wine", or a single letter. This is texture — it should not be legible at a glance. Never on light backgrounds. Never in event posters where it would compete with information.

Refraction Beam Usage Rules

01Hero sections — the primary statement moment
02Section transitions — entering a new context
03Special emphasis — drops, announcements, closers

The spectrum represents light entering the space — not wallpaper. Never behind dense text blocks. The power comes from restraint: when the beams appear, they should feel like something arriving, not something ambient.

Brand Truth
The anti-wine-club wine club.

Every other natural wine brand is trying to be the coolest person in the room. Wine Wankers is the reason the room exists. It's not for people who already know their wine — it's for people who want to. No gatekeeping. No jargon. No pretence.

Just good wine, people who want to drink it, and a community that grows because showing up feels better than not showing up.

09 Brand Scaling

Across the ecosystem.

How this direction applies to the three related entities. Each has a distinct treatment — no wholesale adoption, no visual confusion.

Primary Brand
Wine Wankers

The full Spectrum direction applies here. Dark backgrounds dominate for event marketing and digital presence. The refraction bars appear as signature elements on posters, ticketing pages, and email headers. Yellow is the primary CTA colour. Instrument Serif headlines, Newsreader body, JetBrains Mono labels. This is the parent brand that could theoretically outgrow any single venue.

Online Store
Chasing Rainbows Wine Co.

A sibling brand sharing the Spectrum DNA but shifting to light mode for e-commerce usability. Aged Paper backgrounds, Wine Stain accents on product cards and category headers. The refraction bars appear as a subtle header strip — the spectrum gradient at reduced opacity, top of page. Typography stays consistent. The name already contains the rainbow — this direction makes that connection explicit and visual.

Host Venue
The End Bar

The End has its own identity and shouldn't adopt Wine Wankers' visual system wholesale. When promoting Wine Wankers events, The End uses a co-branded lockup: their existing aesthetic plus the refraction bars as a "Presented by Wine Wankers" element. Event nights can feature printed spectrum elements that feel additive, not intrusive. Contextual only.

07 Voice & Messaging

Anti-pretentious positioning.

Wine Wankers occupies the unclaimed centre: LGBTQ+ community × natural wine culture × local IRL events. No competitor holds this intersection.

Anti-snob, not anti-expert

Disarms pretension. Does not dismiss knowledge — rejects gatekeeping. Warm, curious, slightly irreverent, never smug.

Community-first language

Lead with people, not product. "The room", "The Regulars", "weird people" — these are the story. Wine is the vehicle, belonging is the product.

09 Final Recommendation

The position.

This direction works because it takes something Nick has already been doing unconsciously — the rainbow in every post, the rainbow on the bar wall, the Chasing Rainbows store name — and gives it visual structure. It doesn't impose a new identity; it names the one that already exists.

The visible spectrum is universal enough to hold "suits, hipsters, and everyone in between" without collapsing into either end. It's scientifically grounded — light through glass — but emotionally warm: celebration, joy, discovery. It differentiates from every other natural wine brand in Brisbane while remaining genuinely inclusive.

Implement this. It's already yours.
